01 · Context · Who it's for

Two kinds of players, one app.

Both groups share the booking flow, then split into play and practice.

PlayThe weekend player

Books a lane with friends

  • Wants a venue nearby at the right time
  • Compares price and amenities quickly
  • Books for the whole group and splits the plan in chat
TrainThe training player

Practises with the bowling machine

  • Wants specific deliveries: pace, spin, length, swing
  • Repeats sessions and tracks progress
  • Watches replays to improve technique
02 · Problem

Booking and training both happened off the app.

Before One6, a player couldn't see what was free, pay ahead or prepare a bowling-machine session.

✕

Booking needed a phone call

Players had to call or message the venue to ask which lanes were free.

✕

No view of availability

Without live slots, back-and-forth messages and double bookings were easy.

✕

Paying at the desk

Payment on arrival meant a no-show left an empty, unpaid lane.

✕

Machine set-up ate lane time

Configuring 10+ settings per over on site used up the time players paid for.

Evidence from the project
  • The brief asked for discovery, booking, payment and machine control in one app.
  • Playo, the closest competitor, already offers online booking, so players expect it.
  • The bowling machine exposes 10+ settings per over: arm, type, length, speed, swing, direction, level and randomisation.

04 · Reasoning

The options I weighed, and why.

For each key screen I compared at least two approaches. These are the ones that shaped the app.

Decision 01

Picking a time slot

The most-used screen. It has to show availability instantly.

✕ Considered
Select date ▾Select time ▾

Date and time dropdowns

Hides which slots are free until the list is opened, and adds taps for every change.

✓ Chosen

Time chips grouped by part of day

All availability visible at once, booked slots greyed out, one tap to select.

Decision 02

Setting up the machine

Each over has 10+ settings, and players repeat them often.

✕ Considered
Arm type ▾Bowler type ▾Length ▾Speed ▾

A dropdown for every setting

Opening and closing 10+ menus per over is slow and hides the options.

✓ Chosen

Segmented buttons and a speed slider

Every option visible, one tap to change, plus "Copy to all overs".

Decision 03

Booking several days

Regular players practise across the week.

✕ Considered

Book one day at a time

Players would repeat the whole flow and checkout for every day.

✓ Chosen
From 5 SepTo 10 Sep

A from–to date range

Pick the range once, choose slots, pay once.

Decision 04

Booking for friends

One person usually books for the group.

✕ Considered

Separate accounts for everyone

Friends would need to install and register before anyone could book.

✓ Chosen

Sub-profiles under one account

The booker adds friends once and plays with them every time.

Design decisions

Six moments that shaped the product.

Every screen below is from the shipped design file.

01 · Onboarding

Show the game first, then ask for details.

The intro screens sell what the app does before asking for anything. Sign-up is a phone number and OTP, with no passwords to forget.

  • Skill level as a 5-step slider, from Beginner to Master, used later to match games
  • Sub-profiles so one account can book and play for friends or family
  • Short forms with only the fields the booking needs
02 · Venue discovery

Photo, price and a Book button on every card.

Home leads with large venue photos because players pick a ground by how it looks and what it costs. The venue page then answers every question a player would otherwise call to ask.

  • Price per hour and a Book action right on the card
  • Filters for sport, availability, favourites and offers
  • Venue page covers hours, map, offers, sports, amenities, lanes, rules and cancellation
03 · Check slots

Slots you can read in one glance.

Times are grouped into morning, afternoon and evening, the way players think about their day. Booked slots are greyed out, selected ones turn orange, and the total updates as you tap.

  • Date range picker for booking several days at once
  • A legend that explains booked slots without extra text
  • A sticky bar with slot count, price and a Proceed to pay button that only turns on once you've picked a slot
04 · Checkout & bookings

No surprises at the payment step.

Checkout shows the booking amount, tax and total on separate lines, with contact details pre-filled from the profile. UPI comes first because it's how most players in India pay.

  • Price breakdown: ₹1,800 + ₹30 tax = ₹1,830
  • UPI, card and netbanking in one list
  • My Bookings links each booking to "Program session", tying booking to training
05 · The bowling machine

Turning a control panel into a few taps.

This is what sets One6 apart. Each over can be configured with arm, bowler type, length, speed, swing or seam, direction, level and random variation.

Bowler type
Length
Interactive sketch of the delivery a player sets up in the app

Over by over, without repeating yourself.

Players set up an over once with segmented controls and a speed slider, then copy it to every over or change just one. Saved players and session history mean a coach can rerun a session in seconds.

  • Segmented choices instead of dropdowns: every option visible, one tap each
  • Speed from 50 to 150 km/h on a labelled slider
  • "Copy to all overs" plus randomisation toggles for match-like practice
  • Session history with quick edit and delete per session
06 · Play together

Reasons to come back next week.

Booking gets someone in the door once. The Connect tab and profile give them a reason to return: games to join, friends to beat and goals to hit.

  • Create Game with public or invite-only access and advanced settings for level and format
  • Leaderboard with Overall and Playpals tabs, so players compete with their own circle
  • Monthly goals, reputation badges and stats on the profile
Visual system

Floodlight orange, night-match purple.

Orange marks every action. Purple carries the brand and offers. Everything else stays neutral so photos of the grounds stand out.

06 · Validation

How I checked the design.

I designed for the moments things go wrong, and worked flow by flow with the developer.

!Edge cases designed for

  • Booked slots can't be selected
  • "Proceed to pay" stays off until a slot is picked
  • OTP resend timer, plus a "Send to mail" fallback
  • Empty states for events, deals and bookings
  • Cancellation policy shown before booking

✓How it was checked

  • Walked the developer through each flow so every screen was feasible before build
  • Shared the booking flow first, then refined it while the developer built
  • Compared builds against the designs before launch

?What I'd test next

  • Time a first booking with 5 new players
  • Ask beginners to set up an over without help
  • Find where people drop off between slot and payment

No formal usability study was run within the timeline, so the tests above are the next step rather than results.

08 · Reflection

What I'd do differently.

Three lessons from designing and shipping One6 in under three weeks.

01

Scope is a design tool

Locking the booking flow early let design and development run in parallel without rework.

02

Show every option

For the machine settings, visible segmented choices beat hidden dropdowns for speed.

03

Next, I'd measure

I'd track drop-off between slot selection and payment, and test the machine setup with first-time players.

With more time or better evidence
  • Run a quick usability test of the machine set-up with first-time players
  • Add analytics on drop-off between slot selection and payment
  • Add a short guided tip the first time a player programs a session
