04 · Reasoning

The options I weighed, and why.

Three choices that decide whether the marketplace works.

Decision 01

Choosing a mentor

Learners compare before booking.

✕ Considered

A plain list

Learners have to open every profile.

✓ Chosen

Cards ranked by match

Match %, rating, price and availability up front.

Decision 02

Booking a session

Date, time, service and a note.

✕ Considered
Step 1 of 4: date

A four-page checkout

Changing the date means going back.

✓ Chosen

One page, live summary

Every choice updates the summary instantly.

Decision 03

Admin queues

Admins review items all day.

✕ Considered

Table that opens new pages

Admins lose their place every time.

✓ Chosen

Queue plus side panel

Evidence and actions open beside the list.

06 · Validation

How I checked the design.

I designed for the moments things go wrong.

!Edge cases designed for

  • Booked times can’t be picked
  • Free cancellation shown before booking
  • Failed payout and dispute states

✓How it was checked

  • Walked one booking end to end
  • Every admin queue has list, detail and action
  • Flows tested in the built front end

?What I'd test next

  • Time a first booking
  • Mentors set availability unaided
  • One dispute, report to resolution

Not launched yet, so these tests are next steps, not results.

08 · Reflection

What I’d do differently.

Designing the marketplace, not just the screens.

01

Map the hand-offs first

A booking touches all three roles.

02

Admin is product too

Queues and logs keep the marketplace safe.

03

Test before launch

Real learners on booking, real mentors on availability.

With more time or better evidence
  • Usability test booking with 5 learners
  • Add analytics on search to booking
