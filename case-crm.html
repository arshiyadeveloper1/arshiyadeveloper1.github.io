01 · Context · Who uses it

Every team, one workspace.

Sales, finance, academics and HR each needed their own tools, but the same leads, people and payments moved between them.

Sales agents Team leads & managers Finance Academic team HR & admin
02 · Problem

Leads, calls, payments and people lived in different places.

Each team worked from its own tools, so the same lead or person had to be tracked again and again.

✕

Leads arrived everywhere

WhatsApp, Meta ads, the website and spreadsheets, with no single list to work from.

✕

Agents dialled by hand

Choosing who to call next was guesswork, and calls were hard to review.

✕

Payments sat apart

Links, approvals and EMIs weren't tied to the lead who paid.

✕

Every team had its own tool

Academics, finance and HR couldn't share the same data.

Evidence from the project
  • The CRM needed Google Sheets and Meta Lead Ads integrations, showing where leads lived before.
  • Every lead records its source (WhatsApp, direct, campaigns), because the team needed to know where leads came from.
  • 44 features across sales, finance, academics and HR had to fit into one mobile product.
What success looks likeOne list of leadsFaster callingPayments tied to leadsOne login for every team

The challenge: fit 40+ features on a phone without burying the ones agents use every minute.

04 · Reasoning

The options I weighed, and why.

For the most-used parts of the CRM I compared at least two approaches before choosing one.

Decision 01

Navigating 44 features

Agents switch between a few areas dozens of times a day.

✕ Considered

One long side menu

Agents would scroll past HR and finance to reach leads, every time.

✓ Chosen

Grouped menu + bottom bar

Everything is grouped by team, and the five daily areas are one tap away.

Decision 02

Leads on a phone

Desktop CRMs show leads as wide tables.

✕ Considered

A desktop-style table

Columns get cut off on mobile, with no room for actions.

✓ Chosen

Cards that end in a Call button

Status, source and owner at a glance, with the next action built in.

Decision 03

Who to call next

Agents work through hundreds of leads.

✕ Considered

A list sorted by date

Agents pick at random, and hot leads wait.

✓ Chosen

Smart Caller with a score and queue

The best next lead is on screen, with an auto dialer one tap away.

Decision 04

Where analytics opens

Managers check analytics between calls.

✕ Considered

A dashboard of charts

Lots to read before finding the actual problem.

✓ Chosen

The lead funnel first

Shows where leads drop off, then lets managers dig into trends.

Design decisions

Six problems, six answers.

Every screen below is from the design file.

01 · Navigation

44 features, two ways in.

A grouped side menu holds everything, while a bottom bar keeps the five most-used areas one tap away: Home, Leads, Deals, Tasks and More.

  • Menu grouped by team: Lead management, Content, Users, Financial, HRMS and more
  • Bottom navigation for the screens agents open all day
  • A dark theme that stays comfortable on long calling shifts
02 · Leads

Every lead card ends in an action.

Agents scan dozens of leads a day. Each card shows status, source, owner and last update, with a Call button at the bottom. The detail page puts calling, payment links and support side by side.

  • Status chips like Fresh and Hot lead, readable at a glance
  • Quick comment tags (Interested, Callback, Busy) instead of typing
  • Reminders with priority, so follow-ups never slip
03 · Calling

From scrolling lists to a calling cockpit.

Smart Caller puts the next best lead on screen with its score and attempts, and starts the auto dialer in one tap. Managers monitor dialer agents, and the AI voice agent can call leads on its own.

  • Lead score and a priority queue, so agents call the right person next
  • Calls made versus the daily target, always visible
  • Agent health at a glance: calls, recordings, devices and pending reviews
04 · Analytics

Answers, not just charts.

Lead analytics opens on the funnel, so a manager sees where leads drop off before digging into trends, sources or the full table.

05 · Money & programs

Payments and programs in the same flow as sales.

Once a lead says yes, the agent sends a payment link from the lead itself. Finance sees amount, status and approval on one card, and the academic team manages courses and programs with price and duration up front.

  • Payment cards with clear states such as Expired, Full and Approval
  • Courses listed with price and duration for quick quoting
  • Jumbo programs that bundle batches, with display order and status
06 · Admin & HR

The back office, on the same phone.

Admins control who sees what, module by module. Meetings are booked against real host availability, and every employee can track their own attendance.

  • Module-level permissions with simple on and off switches
  • Meeting scheduler that shows free slots per host
  • Attendance summary (present, late, absent, leave) with a full log

06 · Validation

How I checked the design.

A CRM fails at its edges, so I designed the empty, error and permission states alongside the main flows.

!Edge cases designed for

  • "No follow-ups scheduled today" and "No data for this period"
  • "No caller-ID numbers found", with a clear next step to fix it
  • Payment links that expire, with approval states
  • Locked core modules in permissions
  • Search, filter and sort on every long list

✓How it was checked

  • Designed a calibration view for Smart Caller that compares predicted and actual connect rates by score band, so the team can check if lead scoring works
  • Kept one card, chip and list pattern across 120+ screens for consistency

?What I'd test next

  • Time how long agents take from opening a lead to calling
  • Check managers can find the biggest funnel drop in under a minute
  • Ask each team to complete their top task without help

No formal usability study was run within the timeline, so the tests above are the next step rather than results.

08 · Reflection

What I'd do differently.

Designing every module myself meant the same patterns had to work from a sales call to a payroll run.

01

Patterns scale, screens don't

Reusing a few strong patterns is what made 120+ screens buildable.

02

Design for the busiest user

Sales agents shaped the main flows. Everyone else benefits from that speed.

03

Next, I'd measure

I'd track calls per hour and time from lead to payment, to see where the flow still slows teams down.

With more time or better evidence
  • Shadow sales agents for a day to validate the calling flow
  • Measure calls per hour and time from lead to payment
  • Test the permissions screens with admins before adding more modules
